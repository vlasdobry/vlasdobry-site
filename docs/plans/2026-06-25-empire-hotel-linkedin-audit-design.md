# Empire Hotel LinkedIn Audit — Design

**Date:** 2026-06-25

## Goal

Prepare a fact-checked LinkedIn hotel teardown focused on one clear problem: the Empire Hotel rooms page lists nine room categories but has no H1 or H2 headings; room names begin at H3.

## Audience and message

Audience: independent hotel owners, general managers, and hotel marketing specialists in English-speaking markets.

Core message: a visually complete rooms page can still send a weak hierarchy signal to search engines. The post must explain the issue in business language without claiming unverified ranking or revenue losses.

## Format

- English LinkedIn post, concise and native-sounding.
- Five-slide square carousel, light theme, 1080x1080.
- One problem only; secondary findings stay in audit notes.
- Website link appears only in the first comment, with UTM parameters.
- Deliverables: audit notes, post Markdown, LinkedIn-ready plain text, carousel HTML, PDF, and individual slide images.

## Slide structure

1. Hook: nine room types, zero H1/H2 headings.
2. Evidence: page hierarchy starts directly with nine H3 room names.
3. Why it matters: Google receives no page-level heading defining the page and no section-level grouping.
4. Concrete fix: one H1, one H2, room names kept as H3.
5. Takeaway and CTA to the free SEO/GEO Health Score.

## Fact policy

Use only facts verified from the official Empire Hotel website on 2026-06-25. Do not state that the defect causes a ranking drop, lost bookings, or a specific financial loss. Explain it as a clarity and crawl-context problem. Preserve positive facts where relevant: unique title and description, canonical URL, room booking links, and existing Hotel schema on the homepage.

## Improvement over previous audits

- Five slides instead of six to eight.
- One memorable issue rather than a long technical checklist.
- No invented repair time or financial impact.
- Evidence and corrected hierarchy shown visually.
- Short post copy; carousel carries the proof.
